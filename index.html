Copper Repipe Specialists

Welcome to Southern California copper repiping information.

Homes and commercial properties with aging water pipes sometimes need plumbing repairs or full repiping. Copper repiping involves replacing older water supply pipes with new copper piping. PEX is another common material used for repiping projects.

Plumbing projects vary in size and complexity. A qualified plumber can inspect existing supply lines, explain suitable materials, and discuss work involved before a homeowner decides whether to proceed.

Common Plumbing Services

Depending on the property and the provider, plumbing work may involve:

  • Inspection of water pressure and supply lines
  • Repair or replacement of leaking pipes
  • Copper or PEX repiping
  • Evaluation of discolored water and corrosion
  • General residential plumbing repairs

What is Copper Repiping?

Copper repiping is the process of replacing existing plumbing pipes in a home or business. Many older structures were fitted with galvanized steel pipes. Over time, some galvanized pipes may corrode internally. This can restrict water flow, contribute to discolored water, and lead to leaks.

Replacement options can include copper or other code-permitted materials such as PEX. The best choice depends on the local plumbing code, water conditions, budget, and the building's layout.

Homeowners, You May Need Repiping If...

  • You see a dramatic drop in water pressure. Washing machines may fill more slowly, hoses can have weaker spray, and showers may lose pressure.
  • Tap water appears discolored due to rust. Persistent discoloration deserves an inspection to identify its source.
  • Unusual odor comes from the tap. Water quality concerns should be evaluated rather than automatically attributed to pipes.
  • Pipes begin to leak repeatedly. Multiple leaks in aging plumbing can warrant discussion of larger replacement work.

A plumber can test the system and recommend whether localized repairs or broader repiping may be appropriate.

Why Choose Copper?

Copper has long been used for interior plumbing. It is relatively lightweight, durable when properly installed and suitable for the water chemistry, and does not rust like steel. Material performance and manufacturer warranties vary.
